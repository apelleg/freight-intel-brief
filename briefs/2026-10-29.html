🚛 Uber Freight Intel Brief — 2026-10-29

Read time: ~4 minutes

⚡ Today's Top Signal

Iran's IRGC launched a fourth wave of Hormuz mine warfare today, with three more tankers struck and a vessel burning near Khasab confirmed by UKMTO — Brent crude hit $115.27/bbl and OPEC+ meets in three days. U.S. diesel is already above $6.20/gallon, making this the most acute fuel-surcharge environment of the freight cycle. For Uber Freight, the immediate exposure is two-sided: managed-transportation customers on diesel-denominated FSC contracts face material Q4 cost surprises, while tight truckload capacity (van L/T 9.51, linehaul +33% YoY) limits spot alternatives if contracted carriers start passing through emergency surcharges.

🏢 Competitor Moves

  • Kodiak Robotics: Announced first commercial deal to supply driverless trucks — not operate them — for DTL Transport on Dallas–El Paso and Dallas–Atlanta highway routes (Oct 28 — TechCrunch). Kodiak pivots from being a carrier to a pure AV technology supplier, putting it in a distinct model from Aurora's per-mile DaaS. Implication for UF: Two AV models now competing for carrier adoption — Aurora's managed DaaS (Uber Freight is a distribution partner via Hirschbach) vs. Kodiak's hardware-supply model; UF needs to clarify whether it aggregates both or bets on one before binding DaaS terms close. Confidence: High
  • C.H. Robinson / RXO: Q3 2026 investor call (Oct 28) confirmed deal financing on track; RXO shareholder vote targeted Q4 2026, combined entity expected to close H1 2027 at $25B+ enterprise value. Q3 financials show CHR has the balance sheet to absorb integration costs (revenue $7.4B, +29.5% YoY, adj EPS $4.80). Implication for UF: The 12–18 months of post-close integration uncertainty is a product and sales window — enterprise shippers evaluating managed transportation will be less receptive to CHR pitches during the RXO system-merge phase; UF's Transplace platform stability becomes a tangible differentiator. Confidence: High (SEC 425 filing, Oct 2026)

🔧 Freight Tech & AI

  • F3 FreightTech 25 final — AI automation displaces AV at the top: The 2027 FreightTech 25 list (announced Oct 26 at F3 Chattanooga, now concluded) placed Highway #1 for a third consecutive year, but GenLogs moved to #2 and HappyRobot to #3 — both AI orchestration/automation platforms, displacing the AV-heavy top-3 of prior years. Five AI automation companies entered the top-25 for the first time. The market is no longer rating AV as the primary innovation driver; AI agents operating across the brokerage-carrier-shipper workflow are now the leading edge. (Oct 26–28 — FreightWaves F3)
  • Kodiak asset-light AV model: Kodiak's DTL deal (see Competitor Moves) is the first instance of an AV startup selling the driverless truck itself to a carrier rather than charging per mile. This creates a new cost-of-capacity model: carriers that buy/lease Kodiak trucks amortize the AV stack as capex, competing differently against Aurora's opex-per-mile DaaS. Freight technology platforms and TMS vendors will need to handle both pricing models. (Oct 28 — TechCrunch)

📋 Regulatory Watch

  • FMCSA 9-rule pipeline for Q4: FMCSA's acting administrator told Land Line Media (Oct 28) the agency expects to publish approximately 9 rules before year-end — ending months of regulatory quiet. Probable topics include the AV operations framework, ELP out-of-service enforcement (comment period closed Oct 9 with 1,800+ submissions), and HOS updates. Motor carriers and TMS vendors should prepare for a compliance-update sprint in Q4. (Oct 28 — Land Line Media)

💡 CPO Lens: 3 Things Worth Discussing with Your Team

  1. Hormuz & FSC exposure in managed transportation: With diesel >$6.20/gallon and a 4th wave of Hormuz mine attacks today sending Brent to $115, how are Uber Freight's Transplace-managed customers structured for fuel surcharges heading into Q4 — are any contracts capped or indexed in ways that compress UF's margin if carriers invoke emergency fuel provisions?
  2. AV model choice — aggregator vs. partner: Kodiak's hardware-supply deal and Aurora's DaaS per-mile model represent two incompatible AV cost structures. Before finalizing Hirschbach DaaS binding terms, has the product team defined whether Uber Freight's platform should be AV-model-agnostic (broker capacity from both) or a committed DaaS distribution partner — and what's the TMS integration work each path requires?
  3. CHR+RXO integration window — is UF ready to capture the opportunity? The combined CHR+RXO entity won't be functionally integrated until late 2027 or 2028, and enterprise managed-transportation RFPs that go out in Q1–Q2 2027 will land in the middle of that chaos. What's the specific product and commercial play UF needs ready in the next 90 days to be the credible alternative for shippers re-evaluating managed transportation?
