## 🚛 Uber Freight Intel Brief — 2026-10-23
**Read time: ~4 minutes**

### ⚡ Today's Top Signal
A stark Q3 earnings divergence is crystallizing among US truckload carriers: Knight-Swift beat guidance ($0.83 adjusted EPS vs. $0.71–0.77 guide) while Werner pre-announced a sharp miss ($0.10–0.11 GAAP EPS vs. ~$0.36 consensus), citing $0.18/share in diesel, driver, and insurance costs. This isn't a single-company outlier — 16+ trucking companies filed for bankruptcy from Sept. 18 through Oct. 21, and diesel now ranks as carriers' #1 operating cost for the first time in ATRI's 22-year history. For Uber Freight, the implication is concrete: marginal capacity is exiting faster than load volumes are declining, which means Q4 spot availability will be tighter than current load-to-truck ratios suggest and shippers still buying coverage week-to-week face a sharply higher cost floor heading into peak season.

### 🔧 Freight Tech & AI
- **Volumes Down 15%, Spot Rates Up 47% — The Capacity Erosion Signal:** FreightWaves reports truckload accepted volumes fell ~15% from summer peaks while the National Truckload Index held near $3.44–3.55/mi (+47% YoY). The disconnect is unambiguous: rates are being driven by supply-side capacity exits, not demand. For AI freight-matching and pricing engines, this means demand-side volume signals are a lagging and misleading indicator of available capacity — carrier health, compliance enforcement, and financial stress signals are the leading indicators. ### 🗂️ TMS & Managed Transportation
- **Intermodal Surge Creating Enterprise TMS Workflow Gap:** CHR's October Edge Report documents the structural shift: enterprise TMS systems still treat intermodal as a manual exception workflow — a shipper or planner initiates the mode — but the 30%+ TL-intermodal rate spread and emerging routing guide compliance failures mean intermodal should be the automatic recommendation on eligible lanes. Rail carriers are repositioning equipment and adding chassis but spot-market freight still faces longer lead times in October, especially in the Northeast and Chicago. The TMS platform that builds this as a default optimization lever — not an override — wins the 4PL RFP. ([Oct 2026 — CHR Edge Report Intermodal](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-us/resources/insights-and-advisories/north-america-freight-insights/oct-2026-freight-market-update/intermodal/), [Oct 22, 2026 — Detroit News](https://detroitnews.com/story/business/2026/10/22/railroads-pick-up-business-from-truckers-as-diesel-costs-soar/92382981007))

### 💡 CPO Lens: 3 Things Worth Discussing with Your Team

1. **Modal Optimization as a Transplace Default:** With TL-intermodal rate spread at 30%+ and routing guide compliance collapsing, should Transplace proactively recommend intermodal on eligible 550–1,500 mile lanes — not as a shipper-initiated override but as an automated optimization workflow? What's the priority and realistic timeline to ship this in Q1 2027, and does UF have the rail carrier relationships to execute it at the volume levels Q4 demand requires?

2. **Carrier Financial Health as a Live Signal:** Werner's Q3 miss demonstrates that diesel is causing real cash-flow strain at mid-tier carriers, not just margin compression — and 16 bankruptcies in 5 weeks confirms the exit cycle is running. Does UF have a carrier financial health score or early-warning system that flags at-risk network partners before a Q4 capacity failure? Payment velocity, insurance lapse signals, and bankruptcy predictor models would make UF's reliability a differentiator none of the major peers have productized.

3. **Kodiak Engagement for 2027 AV Diversification:** Kodiak will launch the industry's first truly unsupervised (no observer) driverless long-haul service by year-end, then scale throughout 2027. UF's primary AV partnership is with Aurora, whose commercial deployments still have an in-cab observer. Should UF be in early commercial dialogue with Kodiak for 2027 brokerage or 4PL managed transportation lanes — both to diversify AV capacity sources and to build UF's position as the preferred 4PL on the AV stack before Kodiak locks in exclusive commercial relationships?
