⚡ Today's Top Signal

New Section 301 forced-labor tariffs went live July 24 across 60+ economies — and the ocean in-transit exemption expires tomorrow, July 28, at midnight. This is landing on top of what NRF projects as the highest-ever monthly U.S. import volume (2.47M TEU in July) as retailers raced to beat potential August tariffs. For Uber Freight's Transplace business, this is a live operational test: managed transportation clients with ocean cargo still in customs need proactive, TMS-level alerts today to avoid absorbing unexpected duties — a platform differentiation moment that the market will remember.

📋 Regulatory Watch

  • Section 301 forced-labor tariffs effective July 24: New 10–12.5% duties on imports from 60+ economies — including Canada, Mexico, China, India, Vietnam, EU, UK, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and others — took effect July 24, replacing the expiring Section 122 global 10% surcharge. The in-transit exemption for ocean cargo (loaded before July 24) expires midnight July 28; after tomorrow, no mode qualifies for relief. (July 24, 2026 — Baker McKenzie; July 24, 2026 — Crane Worldwide)
  • USMCA: US-Mexico in third round, Canada sidelined: US and Mexico held their third bilateral USMCA review round in Mexico City last week; Canada remains excluded and now faces 50% Section 338 tariffs on a wide range of goods (effective July 21). The July 1 extension deadline having passed, a 10-year wind-down clock is now running. Cross-border freight dynamics are bifurcated: US-Mexico corridors actively renegotiated; US-Canada lanes carry elevated, escalating tariff risk. (July 21, 2026 — U.S. News)
