**Read time: ~4 minutes**

### ⚡ Today's Top Signal
J.B. Hunt's Q3 2026 earnings (Oct 15) and Uber Freight's own Q4 market report (Oct 18) converge on the same message: the capacity window is closing. JB Hunt's intermodal volumes surged 20% year-over-year with a >30% TL-to-intermodal rate spread actively pulling shippers into structured rail programs, while UF's own analysis rates truckload and Mexico as "high exposure" for Q4 peak. ### 💡 CPO Lens: 3 Things Worth Discussing with Your Team

1. **Intermodal displacement:** JB Hunt's intermodal operating income grew 31% YoY on a 30%+ rate spread advantage over spot TL — shippers resetting Q4 contracts this month have a clear financial incentive to move freight to rail. Should UF build or partner for an intermodal booking layer in its brokerage product to retain shipper wallet share as TL-to-rail conversion accelerates, or does the strategy assume UF captures the TL spend that doesn't convert?

2. **Mexico before October 29:** UF's own Q3 report rates Mexico TL as high exposure, and CANACAR's estimate of 25,000+ sidelined B-1 drivers could constrain cross-border TL supply simultaneously with the Oct 29 USMCA bilateral tariff deadline creating demand uncertainty. Has the carrier procurement team stress-tested this dual supply/demand shock scenario for Laredo, El Paso, and Nogales corridors — and does UF have anchor carrier relationships deep enough to hold coverage?

3. **Transplace 4PL fuel architecture:** EIA diesel stands at $6.382/gal with structural Hormuz shortages projected through 2027. Are the FSC passthrough mechanisms in UF's 4PL managed transportation contracts calibrated for a $6–7/gal diesel environment — or do they reflect a pre-2026 baseline — and should the Transplace product team build dynamic fuel cost modeling as a shipper-facing planning tool for the next 12 months?
