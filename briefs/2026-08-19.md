## 🚛 Uber Freight Intel Brief — 2026-08-19
**Read time: ~4 minutes**

### ⚡ Today's Top Signal
Canada's Section 338 tariffs took effect at 12:01am ET today — the first-ever U.S. invocation of the 1974 Trade Act's Section 338, imposing 50% duties on ~$20B in Canadian goods (autos, dairy, alcohol, lumber, cement, furniture, textiles, machinery) with USMCA protections explicitly voided. CBP's entry-date trigger means goods already in transit pay full duties the moment they clear customs today, creating immediate triage work across US-Canada corridors. For Uber Freight, this is both a brokerage surge moment (shippers need reroutes and nearshore alternatives fast) and a Transplace 4PL validation event — tariff-triggered network reoptimization at enterprise scale is exactly the managed-transport value proposition.

### 📋 Regulatory Watch
- **Section 338 in effect:** CBP enforcing 50% duties on ~$20B in Canadian goods as of 12:01am ET August 19. Entry-date trigger: in-transit goods pay full duties at clearance. FTZ privileged-foreign status is the one legal relief valve for shippers who declared before the effective date. No USMCA carve-out. No sunset clause. CVSA Brake Safety Week (August 23–29) lands 4 days later, creating sequential capacity compression on the same US-Canada corridors already under tariff stress.
