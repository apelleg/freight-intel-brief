### 📋 Regulatory Watch
- **Section 301 forced-labor tariff: full enforcement starts today (July 28):** The in-transit exemption that allowed goods already in transit at the July 24 enforcement date to clear without penalty has now expired. Every container arriving at US ports from this point forward is subject to the full Section 301 tariff structure — near-term impact will show in port drayage congestion and intermodal repricing over the next 72 hours. ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com), July 28, 2026)
- **Canada Section 338 — August 19 confirmed effective date:** The 50% tariff on select Canadian imports is T-22 days away; USMCA provides no exemption. Affected freight categories include automotive parts, softwood lumber, and processed agricultural products — all high-volume lanes for Uber Freight's managed transportation book. ([Crane Worldwide](https://www.craneww.com/knowledge-center/trade-advisory-notices/trade-advisory-new-section-338-tariffs-add-50-duties-on-certain-canadian-imports-effective-august-19-2026/) / [Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/three-strikes-new-50-tariffs-to-hit-certain-canadian-goods-and-usmca-wont-save-you/), July 2026)

1. **AI brokerage margin benchmark:** CHR's Q2 results tomorrow are the first public data point on whether AI automation is translating to gross margin expansion during a rising-rate cycle. What's Uber Freight's equivalent internal metric — gross margin per load as a function of automation coverage — and are we positioned to publish a comparable narrative if analysts start benchmarking against CHR's AI story?

What's our carrier reactivation and retention rate for carriers who went spot-only in Q2 — are they rebooking onto Uber Freight's platform as committed capacity, or are we seeing lane-level thinning that will expose us when the next tightening leg hits?
