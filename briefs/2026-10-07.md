## 🚛 Uber Freight Intel Brief — 2026-10-07
**Read time: ~4 minutes**

### ⚡ Today's Top Signal
President Trump signed an executive order today permitting highway use of tax-free red-dyed (off-road) diesel through December 31, deferring the 24.4-cent/gallon federal excise tax with no interest or penalties — the first direct diesel cost relief for truckers since the Hormuz supply shock began. The move saves carriers roughly $61 per 250-gallon fill-up federally (up to $100+ if states follow suit), providing immediate cash-flow relief to small fleets that have been filing for bankruptcy at a record pace. For Uber Freight, the key watch: most FSC agreements are tied to the DOE benchmark diesel price, which tracks standard highway diesel — if carriers begin blending red-dye purchases, actual pump prices may diverge from the benchmark, creating a mismatch in fuel surcharge calculations across UF's contract portfolio.

### 💡 CPO Lens: 3 Things Worth Discussing with Your Team

1. **Fuel Surcharge Methodology Under Red-Dyed Diesel:** Trump's EO creates a parallel diesel pricing track: on-highway red-dye (no 24.4¢/gal federal excise tax) vs. standard highway diesel tracked by the DOE benchmark used in most freight FSC agreements. If carriers blend purchases, actual pump prices may diverge from DOE benchmark prices — creating FSC over/under-recovery across UF's contract portfolio. Does UF's legal and finance team need to audit FSC contract language now, before the Q4 repricing cycle? What's the remediation path if carriers ask for FSC recalibration?

2. **CHR+RXO Competitive Positioning — Product Differentiation Imperative:** C.H. Robinson is framing the $5.8B RXO acquisition around "Lean AI" ($300M synergy target via automation) while simultaneously publishing a multi-year rate forecast that anchors shipper expectations at +30% YoY. Simultaneously, Uber's parent is investing heavily in food delivery (Delivery Hero, ezCater), putting heightened ROI scrutiny on freight's product investment budget. The question for the product/engineering team: where does UF's AI automation *already* have a defensible lead over a combined CHR+RXO, and where is the team still catching up? Amir's first strategic roadmap review should have an explicit CHR response slide.

3. **Flexport MCP as AI-Native Booking Benchmark — Build or Buy?** Flexport's MCP server (launched Oct 6) enables Claude, ChatGPT, and Copilot agents to book freight at negotiated rates without human login — processing 21M tasks/year. UF's brokerage and Transplace TMS are not yet similarly AI-agent accessible. With the Flexport North America webinar happening tomorrow (Oct 8) and CHR building its own AI automation stack post-RXO, the window to establish AI-native booking infrastructure as a competitive differentiator is narrowing. What's the fastest path to a UF equivalent, and should it be built internally or acquired via partnership?
